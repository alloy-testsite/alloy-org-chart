DOLE & regulatory essentials

  • Safety Officer (OSH): Every workplace must designate a DOLE-trained Safety Officer under RA 11058 and its IRR, DOLE D.O. 198-18. For a low-risk office setup with 1–50 workers, Safety Officer 1 (SO1) is the minimum — it can be a collateral duty for an existing HR staffer. Once headcount reaches 10, an OSH Committee (management + worker representation, meeting at least monthly) becomes mandatory.
  • Company nurse / physician: Under Labor Code Art. 157, a full-time registered nurse is required once headcount passes 50 (a trained first-aider suffices below that for non-hazardous office work). A part-time physician and dentist join at 200+ employees. Because BPO seat work is non-hazardous, the physician/dentist can be engaged on retainer rather than full-time.
  • Data Privacy Officer: BPOs are named a "critical sector" under National Privacy Commission rules, so registering a DPO and your data-processing systems with the NPC (RA 10173, NPC Circular 2022-04) is required regardless of headcount. The DPO should sit close to the CEO for independence, even if one person also wears the HR or compliance hat early on.
  • Payroll & labor standards: 13th month pay (PD 851), SSS/PhilHealth/Pag-IBIG employer registration and remittance, holiday and overtime pay, and night-shift differential — +10% for hours between 10pm–6am under Labor Code Art. 86 — matter a lot for a BPO running graveyard shifts for offshore clients.
  • DOLE registration: File an Establishment Report with your DOLE regional/field office within 30 days of starting operations, and keep 201 files and payroll records on hand for inspection.

General guidance, not legal advice — thresholds and required filings can change, so confirm current requirements with DOLE, the NPC, or a Philippine labor lawyer before finalizing headcount plans.

Roles you can combine while small

A day-one team with a handful of seats doesn't need every box filled by a different person. Reasonable combinations for an early-stage BPO:

  • HR Manager can carry the Data Privacy Officer and general compliance duties until the client roster or headcount grows.
  • Finance & Admin Manager can absorb Admin & Facilities and Payroll duties directly, or route payroll to an outsourced bookkeeper.
  • Operations Manager can run Workforce Management on spreadsheets until seat volume justifies a dedicated WFM analyst.
  • A Team Lead can cover QA spot-checks before you hire a dedicated QA Lead — split it out as soon as client SLAs require independent QA.
  • IT can start as a retainer or managed-service arrangement instead of a full-time hire.
